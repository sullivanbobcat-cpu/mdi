## What moved

- **Event contracts are being pulled into swap regulation.** On October 9 the CFTC proposed adding event contracts (sports, politics, culture, weather) to the definition of "swap." The same day, an interim final rule took casino-style products such as sportsbook and casino wagers out of that definition, effective on publication in the Federal Register, with comments due 30 days later.
- **The CFTC laid out its first crypto market structure framework.** On October 5 it released an advance notice for two regimes: Regulation CAM, a narrower, opt-in version of a designated contract market for crypto, and Regulation CTX, which routes leveraged and margined crypto trades through futures commission merchants. Spot trading without leverage stays outside it. The 60-day comment window opens once the notice is published.
- **Tokenized U.S. stocks filed for 24/7 trading.** OKXICE, the 50-50 venture between ICE and OKX, notified the SEC on October 5 of plans for round-the-clock trading in tokenized shares of more than 60 U.S.-listed companies, keeping dividend and voting rights. It relies on the SEC's five-year Innovation Exemption from September, and listed companies get a 30-day window to object.
- **SEC crypto offering rules closed for comment.** Comments on the SEC's proposed Regulation Crypto Assets, which includes a startup exemption and a safe harbor from the investment-contract test, were due October 20.

## Why it matters

The common thread is regulators building market structure through rulemaking because Congress hasn't. The Digital Asset Market Clarity Act, meant to cover crypto spot markets, stalled in the Senate in September, so the CFTC and SEC are filling in their own sides of the line.

For prediction markets, the swap route is the bigger story. It puts event contracts under a federal derivatives framework rather than state gambling law, while carving casino-style products out explicitly. How contracts settle, and who decides the outcome, becomes a regulatory question rather than a platform choice. That's the gap the [event contract settlement comparison](/event-contracts/) on this site tracks.
