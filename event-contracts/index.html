Divergence risk: High — Each venue defines “shutdown” differently. Kalshi requires an actual furlough of non-essential federal workers, explicitly excluding brief funding gaps that do not result in furloughs. Polymarket International’s nearest listed contract bundles the shutdown outcome with the House election winner, making it a fundamentally different binary. CME lists no comparable contract. A short-lived funding lapse that triggers Kalshi’s furlough definition but falls outside Polymarket’s bundled condition—or a brief gap that avoids furloughs entirely—would produce divergent settlements across every active venue. This definitional gap produced inconsistent resolution signals across venues during the January 2026 funding episode.

Divergence risk: Low — The Federal Reserve’s rate announcement is immediate and unambiguous; all binary event contract venues resolve against the same FOMC statement, minimizing definitional risk. The primary residual exposure is timing: if a meeting is unexpectedly postponed, Polymarket US’s Rule 10.3(b) grants the Company discretion to adjust the expiration date, while Kalshi’s ORC would make an equivalent determination, with no guarantee of identical timing. CME’s 30-Day Federal Funds Futures are a different instrument—settling against the realized monthly average rate rather than a cut/hold/hike binary—and cannot be compared directly.

Case study: Cardi B at Super Bowl LX (February 2026)

A split resolution on the same event produced a 74% gap in settlement value between venues.

An event contract on whether Cardi B would perform at Super Bowl LX traded on both Kalshi and Polymarket. When Cardi B made a guest appearance during the halftime show, the question of whether the appearance met each contract’s definition of a qualifying performance was disputed across venues.

Kalshi — Declared the outcome unresolvable under Rule 6.3(c). Settled at last-traded price: YES at $0.26, NO at $0.74.

Polymarket — Resolved YES at $1.00. The appearance was treated as a qualifying performance.

The table below illustrates the settlement gap for a holder of 100 YES contracts at a last-traded price of $0.65 each (total cost: $65.00).

Venue Settlement per contract Total received (100 contracts) Net gain / loss
Polymarket $1.00 $100.00 +$35.00 (+53.8%)
Kalshi $0.26 $26.00 −$39.00 (−60.0%)

The $0.74 gap between a $1.00 payout and a $0.26 payout represents a 74% shortfall relative to full settlement for Kalshi YES holders—on a contract where both venues agreed on the underlying event.
